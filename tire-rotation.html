SERVICE DETAILS

ROTATION IS MAINTENANCE — NOT BALANCING OR ALIGNMENT.

Tire Rotation changes the positions of the tires on the vehicle. Tire Balancing addresses the tire-and-wheel assembly, and Wheel Alignment addresses wheel angles relative to the vehicle. Those services can be related, but they solve different problems. Keeping the pages separate helps you request the service that matches the concern instead of assuming every vibration, pull or wear pattern has the same cause.

SERVICE DETAILS

WHEN TO THINK ABOUT ROTATION.

Follow the vehicle or tire manufacturer’s maintenance guidance rather than one universal mileage rule. Include the vehicle and any wear or vibration concern in the notes so the shop has context before confirming the appointment.

SERVICE CONTEXT

ROTATION WORKS BEST WITH THE RIGHT VEHICLE CONTEXT.

Tire Rotation patterns and maintenance timing can depend on the vehicle and tire setup, so use the vehicle or tire manufacturer’s guidance as the baseline. Follow the manufacturer guidance rather than one universal mileage interval. Send the vehicle information and mention uneven wear, vibration or another concern if you noticed one.

If the concern is vibration rather than maintenance, Tire Balancing may be more relevant. If the vehicle pulls or the steering wheel is off-center, Wheel Alignment is a separate Freedom Drive-only service. Those distinctions help the shop start from the right service.

COMMON QUESTIONS

TIRE ROTATION QUESTIONS.

Why rotate tires?

Rotation changes tire positions on the vehicle to help manage wear over time.

How often should tires be rotated?

Follow the vehicle or tire manufacturer maintenance guidance rather than one universal interval.

Is Tire Rotation the same as Tire Balancing?

No. Rotation changes tire positions; balancing addresses the tire-and-wheel assembly.

Can I request other tire service at the same time?

Yes. Use the notes or the related Tire Services if Balancing, TPMS or another tire concern also needs attention.
